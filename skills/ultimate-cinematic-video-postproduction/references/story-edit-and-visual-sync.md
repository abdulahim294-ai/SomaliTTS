# Story Editing and Semantic Synchronization

## Principle

The image should illustrate the meaning of the narration. Synchronization is semantic and temporal, not merely matching total durations.

## Narration map

For each phrase, record:
- exact words or verified meaning;
- phrase in/out;
- character, object, action, location;
- emotional intent;
- visual evidence required;
- candidate source timecode;
- shot scale and continuity;
- sound cue;
- confidence.

## Shot selection hierarchy

1. Exact shot of described action.
2. Exact character/object shot.
3. Reaction shot with correct continuity.
4. Relevant establishing/context shot.
5. Restrained graphic or text card.

Do not use an unrelated shot just because it is visually attractive.

## Continuity

Check screen direction, character identity, location, time of day, action direction and chronology. Reordering footage is allowed only when it does not change story meaning.

## Pacing

Vary shot length according to information density and emotion. Use shorter cuts for action and controlled longer shots for emotional or explanatory beats. Avoid constant zooms and transitions.

## Outro

Unused footage is not automatically an outro. Inspect it, select coherent shots, construct a deliberate montage and end on a clear visual beat. Do not randomize scenes or accelerate dialogue.
