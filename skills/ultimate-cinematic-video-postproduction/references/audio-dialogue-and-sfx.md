# Dialogue Editing, Source Separation and Sound Design

## Dialogue integrity

Dialogue is the narrative source of truth when designated by the user. Preserve all words and phrase order. Avoid cuts inside phonemes. Use waveform and listening checks around every edit.

## Breath classification

- Loud inhale that distracts: attenuate or remove with short crossfades.
- Quiet natural breath: usually retain.
- Breath-like consonant/fricative: preserve.
- Uncertain sound: preserve rather than risk speech damage.

## Source separation

A mixed source track may contain voice, SFX, music and ambience. Source separation is probabilistic, not perfect. Evaluate transient damage and vocal residue. Gunshots, impacts and sirens are especially vulnerable to artifacts.

Preferred order: isolated stems > clean source mix > tested separation > carefully rebuilt effects > omission with disclosure.

Never describe a source-separated effects stem as pristine without listening/inspection.

## Mix workflow

- Dialogue cleanup before loudness normalization.
- Corrective EQ before compression.
- Conservative denoise.
- De-ess only as needed.
- True-peak limiting after mix.
- Duck music under narration.
- Keep story-critical effects audible without masking speech.
- Check mono compatibility and channel balance.

## Loudness

For speech-led online video, use an approximate integrated target around -16 to -14 LUFS and true peak no higher than -1 dBTP as a starting point. The correct target depends on platform, channel layout and creative intent. Measure rather than guess.
